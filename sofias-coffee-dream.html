The dream is a loop, not a destination

Sofia does not think in departments. She thinks in a circle that has to keep turning: buy well, roast well, serve well, and the guest comes back and pays for the next sack. Hover any station.

Why start here

Every figure in this library measures one hop of this loop

The roasting loss measures the second hop. Revenue per guest measures the third. The fourteen-day return rate measures the fourth. Waiting time at the counter measures whether the third hop damages the fourth. A key figure that cannot be placed on this circle is usually a figure nobody needed.

Where it began

Not with a shop. With a bicycle, an espresso machine and a green umbrella.

The coffee bike is the oldest part of the business, not the newest. Everything else grew out of it: the first coffee house because the queue at the bike got too long, the roastery because buying roasted beans meant somebody else decided how they tasted, the wholesale line because the roastery had capacity left over. That order matters when you look at the data later — the bike is the only sales point with no fixed address, and it is in the location dimension because it was there first, not because someone wanted an edge case.

The value chain

From a cooperative in Peru to a cup on the Ringstraße

Read left to right and it is a supply chain. Read it as a data problem and it is four systems that do not know about each other: an import file, a roasting log, a till, and a wholesale order book. Every one of them calls a kilogram of coffee something different, and two of them are not even sure what a customer is.

Hover any station for its role, its figures and the module that covers it.

The loop worth pointing at. Sofia sells roasted beans to seven bakeries — and buys her cake and pastry from bakeries, partly the same ones. The same business relationship appears once as a customer and once as a supplier, under two different numbers, in two different systems. Nobody planned this; it is what happens when a business grows by saying yes. It is also the cleanest example in the whole case of why master data is a discipline and not a spreadsheet.

Reading the list

These are not four problems. They are one problem and three symptoms.

Pen and paper is the cause. The tax advisor owning the numbers is the consequence — if nobody inside the business can produce a figure, somebody outside will, on their own schedule. Falling revenue and rising complaints are what that looks like from the outside, months later, when it is expensive to fix.

Hover each line for what it actually means in Sofia's week.

The tax advisor is not the villain. His figures are correct, complete and auditable — and they arrive ten weeks after the quarter ends. That is exactly right for the tax office and useless for a decision on a Tuesday morning. The line between the two is the line Reichmann draws through his pyramid: accounting systems are not controlling, they are the floor controlling stands on. If you want the theory behind Sofia's complaint, it is in the Controlling Pyramid module.

The diagnosis

Worth saying out loud before anyone proposes a tool.

Sofia does not work too little. She works more hours than she ever has. The figures she needs already exist — the till writes every line, the roaster logs every batch, the ERP knows every sack from Peru. They are simply in four places, in four shapes, and three weeks old by the time anyone adds them up. That is not an effort problem and it is not a software problem. It is the problem this entire course is about.

The double act

Sofia is not the fool — she is the impatient one

She reads a figure, draws a conclusion and acts, often on the same afternoon. That instinct built the business: nobody cautious straps an espresso machine to a bicycle. It is also what makes her expensive when the figure means something other than what it looks like.

Nguyen does not have better ideas. She has one better habit: before believing a number, she asks what it counts. That question is worth ten thousand euros exactly once — after that, Sofia asks it herself.

Nothing was broken. No figure was wrong, no receipt was lost, and the quarter added up to 145 500 € in both reports. The one sentence

Sofia never wanted a dashboard. She wanted her afternoons back. Every model, every star schema and every scorecard in this library exists for one reason: so that the answer takes a minute instead of a fortnight — and so that one day she can finally say it and mean it. Right, then I'll cycle to Piazza San Marco.
